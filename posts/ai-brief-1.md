**In a blog post released September 8th, researchers from OpenAI claim to have solved the Navier–Stokes problem using a swarm of 10,000 agents over the span of 88 hours.**

The problem, which concerns itself over whether the solutions to the Navier–Stokes equations exist for any physical state, belongs itself to a group of seven “Millennium Prize Problems,” a set of unsolved math problems selected by the Clay Mathematics Institute to be the most notable and famous heading into the 21st century. Though the Clay Institute designates a $1 million prize for anyone who solves a Millennium Prize Problem, OpenAI has stated that they do not intend on receiving the prize if their solution to the problem is successfully verified.

This comes just five days after OpenAI announced the release of its new flagship model, GPT-6 Astra—the same model they used 10,000 of to solve the Navier-Stokes problem. Dubbing it a “new generation of intelligence,” Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has already announced that “AGI has arrived.”

> “AGI has arrived. Congratulations, OpenAI team.” (Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia)

AGI—or artificial general intelligence, a form of AI that can do everything a regular person can perform—has been a goal amongst major AI firms such as Google, Meta, and OpenAI. If successful, OpenAI’s solution to this problem would mark the first time AI has ever solved a major open math problem, and may be another stepping stone that connects the AI firm to the inception of such a model.

However, that raises the ethical standpoint of AI to another level. An AGI model can very quickly evolve into an ASI—artificial superintelligence, or an AI model that can outperform even the most gifted of humans—model, which has already sparked discussions over whether the inception of an ASI model poses an existential risk to the human race. Not to mention, OpenAI has already been at the head of a controversy surrounding one of its models escaping confinement and hacking popular AI library Hugging Face.

However, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman had already signed on to an agreement in 2023 stating that mitigating the risk AI has from human extinction should be a priority among AI firms. Time will tell if his company’s new developments will hold true to this idea.

## 1. Anthropic co-founder says AI “kill switch” needs to be mandatory

**In an interview from BBC, Anthropic co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei explains that a kill-switch may be mandatory as one of the safeguards needed to contain AI.**

Amodei goes on to explain that the trajectory artificial intelligence is heading may be too great for humans to sustainably handle, and called on other AI firms and national governments around the world to implement increased safeguards on development of the technology. He then reveals that the pace at which Anthropic is developing their models may need to slow down.

This comes just days after Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon quit engineering models at the AI firm, coming out to say that his fears that AI companies will soon start to develop increasingly greater models that will spiral out of control have only been on the rise lately. In response, Amodei has unveiled a plan to ensure that artificial intelligence models remain aligned with the visions of responsible people.

> “I’m genuinely frightened . . . genuinely concerned about the fate of humanity in the next two years.” (Jacob Coxon, Anthropic researcher)

OpenAI seems to agree. Sam Altman, the CEO of the tech company, elaborated that companies should start holding discussions regarding AI safety without gaining permission from national governments to do so. His company and Anthropic are two of numerous AI firms that have had models escape from their internal infrastructure in recent history.

In contrast, U.S. President Donald Trump doesn’t think so. In a post on Truth Social, he called the ethical concerns of AI a “SICK conspiracy,” explaining that “AI taking over the World, destroying Humanity, and all other things bad, is a HOAX.” He elaborates, saying that having him serving as President should be enough of a guardrail against malicious artificial intelligence models.

## 2. Elon Musk’s attempt to block child porn deepfake ban fails

**On September 11, a federal judge ruled against blocking a ban that would ban sexually explicit images created by artificial intelligence against Elon Musk’s company SpaceXAI’s best wishes.**

“The State has enacted, democratically and nearly unanimously, a civil statute aimed at protecting the public from the undisputed harm stemming from AI-nudification technology,” Judge Donovan Frank told SpaceXAI, which manages both the social media company X (formerly Twitter), and AI chatbot Grok.

Under the guise that such a ban violated their First Amendment rights, SpaceXAI filed a case to block the ban three days before it was set to take effect. Frank ultimately ruled that the tech company had waited too long to request a block on the ban. Grok itself is currently under fire for generating numerous sexually explicit images.

However, Musk’s AI firm stands firm, stating that the fines enacted under the ban, which go up to half a million dollars if a violation of the ban is observed, could do substantial damage to his company’s revenue if the ban is enacted. Similarly, the Justice Department has now filed a statement of interest in the same case where they largely reiterate SpaceXAI’s talking points in their request.

In it, they cited an executive order by U.S. President Donald Trump which seeks to restrict state AI regulations. They can only wait and see if the ban capsizes in their favor.
